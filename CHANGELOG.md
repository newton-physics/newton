## [1.8.0] - 2026-11-12

### Added

- Add the experimental `newton.solvers.SolverSteel`, which combines Featherstone articulated-body dynamics with a projected Gauss-Seidel solve of contacts with coupled Coulomb friction, optional joint limits (`enable_joint_limits`, off by default) and joint velocity limits, and integrates joint drives implicitly. Passive joint damping (`Model.joint_damping`) applies to prismatic, revolute, ball and D6 DOFs; passive joint springs are not applied yet. The default `solve_mode="matrix_free"` is a fused CUDA solve that implements the optional features subject to their documented combination restrictions; `solve_mode="split"` assembles a dense Delassus matrix per world, runs on CPU and CUDA (pass it explicitly on CPU) and raises when an option that only the matrix-free solve implements is active (`NotImplementedError`, except positive contact torsion on CPU, which raises `ValueError`). Rows beyond the per-world capacities are dropped and reported in `constraint_overflow`, with the `(world_count + 1,)` layout of the reset mask; the solver supports world-masked `reset()` and CUDA graph capture. Contacts that couple a dynamic global body, or a kinematic global articulation with joints, to another world's bodies cannot be solved; they are dropped and flagged, and the constructor warns when the model allows them. Unsupported model features, including enabled MuJoCo equality constraints that were not converted to Newton loop joints or mimic constraints, raise `NotImplementedError` instead of being ignored.

  Support free rigid bodies whose FREE joint has a rotated parent anchor (`joint_X_p`): contact rows and response, free-body velocity limits and the gyroscopic update now use the anchor frame of the joint velocities, so these bodies move as they do with a world-aligned anchor.

  Preserve `body_q` and write `body_qd` in the output state for bodies outside every articulation, such as a jointless kinematic link; they now pass their input state through the step.

  Speed up `SolverSteel`'s fused matrix-free Gauss-Seidel sweep for streamed row metadata, contact torsion and dense friction pairs; the row and reduction order are unchanged. No migration is required.
- Add the experimental `articulated_contact_response="propagation"` and `"propagation_colored"` options to `newton.solvers.SolverSteel` (matrix-free solve only), which solve contacts of articulated bodies as body-space rows with responses from an articulated-body factorization of each tree, `"propagation_colored"` in parallel batches of contacts that share no body; `propagation_same_articulation_rows` for contacts between links of the same articulation; and `propagation_cached_response` with `propagation_cached_response_body_max`, which apply cached per-body responses instead of the per-iteration tree walk. The propagation rows support restitution, contact regularization, warm start, velocity-only iterations, friction patches, PGS drive rows and mimic rows; the split solve and loop-closing joints raise `NotImplementedError`. Both responses support contact torsion with `contact_torsion_device=True` and `velocity_iterations=0`; `"propagation_colored"` also supports contact compliance, without contact torsion, and sleeping, which `"propagation"` rejects. Plain `"propagation"` requires `friction_mode="fixed_normal"`.

  Accept `friction_mode` values other than `"fixed_normal"` with `SolverSteel(articulated_contact_response="propagation_colored")`. Each contact on a propagation row, articulated ones included, solves its normal and sliding rows together with the coupled update the matrix-free solve applies to free-body contacts.

  Preserve grasps in `propagation` and `propagation_colored` when contacting links share a moving ancestor but move mostly through their own joints, such as the pads of a linkage gripper on an arm. The sweep split each such link's whole response by the number of contacting links on the shared ancestor, which can slow friction convergence and allow grasps that `immediate` holds to slip at finite iteration counts. Only the shared joints' share of the response is split now.

  Accept `propagation_same_articulation_rows=True` with `SolverSteel(solve_mode="colored", articulated_contact_response="propagation")`, which solves contacts between two links of one articulation as colored propagation rows with the exact cross response of the two links.
- Add `SolverSteel(deterministic=wp.DeterministicMode.RUN_TO_RUN)`, which reserves constraint rows in a fixed per-world order (joint rows in articulation order, contact rows in contact-buffer order) instead of through atomic counters, and sums split-mode right-hand sides in a fixed order. With identically ordered contacts, for example from `CollisionPipeline(deterministic=True)`, repeated runs are bitwise identical on the same GPU model, driver and Warp build, eagerly and under CUDA graph replay. The default (`None`) inherits `wp.config.deterministic`, which is off unless set. The mode supports every `articulated_contact_response`. An explicit `GPU_TO_GPU` raises, and an inherited one runs `RUN_TO_RUN` with a `NewtonWarning`.

  Avoid races that could change CUDA results from run to run: the mass-matrix fill of free and ball joints, and missing warp synchronization in several PGS solve kernels. A full `SolverSteel.reset()` (`world_mask=None`) also restarts the step cadence of later eager steps, so they repeat a fresh solver's steps.
- Add `SolverSteel(propagation_color_order=...)` for `articulated_contact_response="propagation_colored"`. The default `"contact"` batches contacts in contact-index order as before; `"longest_first"` takes contacts with more rows first, which shortens the colored sweep where one body carries many contacts with different row counts.
- Add `SolverSteel(propagation_constraint_max=...)` to size the propagation row family of the `"propagation"` and `"propagation_colored"` contact responses on its own, so `free_body_constraint_max` no longer has to be large enough for every contact row.
- Add `SolverSteel.register_custom_attributes()`, which registers the PhysX rigid-body USD attributes `physxRigidBody:angularDamping`, `linearDamping`, `maxLinearVelocity`, `maxAngularVelocity` and `maxDepenetrationVelocity` as the `steel:rigid_body_angular_damping`, `steel:rigid_body_linear_damping`, `steel:rigid_body_max_linear_velocity`, `steel:rigid_body_max_angular_velocity` and `steel:rigid_body_max_depenetration_velocity` body attributes (for example `model.steel.rigid_body_angular_damping`), and `physxCollision:minTorsionalPatchRadius` as the `steel:min_torsional_patch_radius` shape attribute that enables contact torsion; the solver reads them every step.
- Add `drive_mode="rows"` to `SolverSteel`, which solves joint drives as projected Gauss-Seidel rows with the PhysX articulation force-drive update together with contacts and limits, and bounds the complete drive reaction by the effort limit, and `fuse_joint_velocity_limits`, which enforces the velocity limits of driven DOFs with an end-of-iteration clamp instead of velocity-limit rows.
- Add an experimental `steel:joint_friction_load_coefficient` joint DOF attribute to `SolverSteel` that grows a DOF's joint friction with the load its joint transmits, as PhysX articulation joint friction coefficients do before PhysX 5.6. The load term is set up only when a coefficient is positive at construction.
- Add automatically selected mass-matrix paths to `SolverSteel`: sparse factors in the fill-free pattern of the kinematic tree for branched articulations of one shared topology, and per-DOF division for articulations whose degrees of freedom are not coupled (bitwise identical to the dense `loop` kernels). The selection is structural, not a performance prediction, and sparse factors can be markedly slower on some topologies. Add the opt-in `parallel_tree` option, which traverses the independent branches of each articulation tree in parallel on CUDA.
- Add experimental implicit compliance of hydroelastic contacts to `SolverSteel` (`contact_compliance`): contacts with positive stiffness are solved as unilateral spring-dampers with the contact damping and friction weight. It requires point friction, which an omitted `friction_anchor_beta` selects (with a warning) when compliance is enabled. Compliant rows are prepared on the device, so steps can be captured in a CUDA graph; `check_constraint_capacity()` reports invalid input from captured steps.

  Accept the experimental `contact_compliance=True` of `newton.solvers.SolverSteel` with `solve_mode="colored"` and `articulated_contact_response="propagation"`. The colored sweep solves compliant contacts with the same device-prepared coefficients and `gamma * lambda` residual term as the immediate response, and the step stays CUDA graph capturable.
- Add experimental opt-in dry joint friction to `SolverSteel`: `enable_joint_friction=True` enforces `Model.joint_friction` of PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL and D6 DOFs as a per-DOF impulse box in the matrix-free solve. Steps check the coefficients on the device, also when captured in a CUDA graph, and `SolverSteel.check_constraint_capacity` reports the worlds that stepped with invalid ones.
- Add experimental, opt-in passive-island sleeping to `SolverSteel` (`enable_sleeping=False` by default). Articulations in contact are joined into islands every step; a supported island that stays below `sleep_linear_threshold` and `sleep_angular_threshold` for `sleep_quiet_time` freezes its published state and, with `sleep_skip_constraints=True`, skips its contact and joint-limit rows, its friction-patch rebuild and its articulated dynamics. External or nonfinite forces, state and gravity changes, contacts with awake or moving bodies, lost support or row capacity, `reset()` of its world and `notify_model_changed()` for its own properties wake it. Sleeping works under CUDA graph capture; it is rejected with `contact_warmstart`, `velocity_iterations` and `solve_mode="split"`. Request `SolverSteel.ObservableKind.BODY_AWAKE` to read each body's awake flag at the end of a step.

  Avoid quadratic per-step contact processing for a sleeping body pair on one GPU thread, so flat objects built from many convex pieces do not stall every world.

  Clear the selected worlds' sleep history on a masked `SolverSteel.reset()` with `enable_sleeping=True`: their state-change baseline now restarts from the reset state and their asleep counters clear.

  Allow experimental `enable_sleeping=True` with `SolverSteel(solve_mode="colored", articulated_contact_response="propagation")`; the coloring and colored sweep leave out the contacts of skipped sleeping islands.

  Skip the propagation trees of sleeping articulations in `SolverSteel(solve_mode="colored", articulated_contact_response="propagation")`, and its whole propagation solve in captured steps where every articulation that can sleep sleeps. No migration is required.
- Add mimic joints, loop-closing BALL joints (including closures to a kinematic body or the world, released, engaged and re-anchored at runtime through `Model.joint_enabled`, `Model.joint_X_p` and `Model.joint_X_c`) and opt-in regularized bilateral pre-elimination (`enable_bilateral_preelimination`) to `SolverSteel`.

  Prevent loop closures from adding angular momentum to a free-floating mechanism while the closure is open: CONNECT rows between two bodies of one articulation that both have a non-kinematic DOF on their path to the root now apply their impulses at the midpoint of the two anchors instead of at each body's own anchor, and `BODY_PARENT_F` reports these forces at the same point.
- Add persistent friction patches, contact regularization, restitution from `ShapeConfig.restitution` (zero by default), contact-identity warm start, velocity-only iterations and gap gates for speculative contacts to `SolverSteel` (`friction_anchor_beta`, `contact_regularization`, `restitution_velocity_threshold`, `contact_warmstart`, `velocity_iterations`, `contact_gap_gate`, `contact_friction_gap_max`; `None` turns either gate off, so `contact_gap_gate=0` keeps touching contacts only). The default `friction_anchor_beta=None` selects friction patches, which hold static loads without creep, in the matrix-free solve and point friction in the split solve; pass `friction_anchor_beta=0` for point friction.

  Release friction-patch anchors when a body slides on a triangle mesh or another footprint with redundant contacts, so it does not slide faster than its friction coefficient allows. Holds that need nearly all of their friction budget can now creep slightly when `iterations` is below the default.

  Prevent restitution on contacts that this step's forces (gravity, a joint drive, a joint force or a body force) press closed faster than `restitution_velocity_threshold`. A contact now bounces only when it already closes faster than the threshold at the start of the step, and it leaves with at least `e` times that step-start closing speed, so a pressed resting contact stays at rest and a landing under a sustained press settles instead of bouncing repeatedly.

  With `contact_warmstart=True` and friction anchors, constrain the contact-matched seed of a friction-patch anchor without patch history to its patch cone; the seed is now projected onto the cone like a carried anchor's.
- Add solver-wide physics overrides to `newton.solvers.SolverSteel` whose defaults leave the model's properties in charge: `contact_friction_scale` (scales the shapes' friction coefficients in the solver's copy of the materials), `contact_friction_position_iterations` (solves tangential friction rows only in the last `k` position iterations; `-1`, the default, solves them in every iteration, and velocity-only iterations always solve them) and `enable_restitution` (`False` turns rebounds off regardless of the shapes' coefficients and lets the split solve and contact compliance accept shapes with positive restitution).
- Add the `steel_determinism_arms` example, which steps one scene in two default and two `SolverSteel(deterministic=wp.DeterministicMode.RUN_TO_RUN)` copies and overlays the two copies of each mode, so diverging copies show as ghosts.
- Add the `steel_sleeping` example, in which four box towers fall asleep as separate islands and a thrown block wakes only the tower it hits.
- Add the experimental `friction_mode` option of `newton.solvers.SolverSteel` (matrix-free solve only), which selects the Coulomb friction update: `"fixed_normal"` (the default), `"bisection"` and `"bisection_desaxce"`, which bisect each contact's normal impulse with the tangent problem re-solved at every probe (the latter with the de Saxce maximum-dissipation bias on free-body contacts), and `"coulomb_newton"`, which solves each free-body contact's Coulomb cone with a bracketed Newton iteration. The non-default modes use point friction; the split solve raises `NotImplementedError` for them.

  Keep `friction_mode="bisection"` and `"bisection_desaxce"` from lifting a resting body by a float32 height step per step, so boxes on zero-gap contacts do not lose contact and rock. The coupled normal-and-friction solve now interpolates the normal impulse within its final bisection bracket, and its zero-load check includes the sliding impulses.

  Honor kinematic-body tangential velocity in matrix-free immediate contacts with `friction_mode="bisection"`, `"bisection_desaxce"` or `"coulomb_newton"`, so friction drags a body resting on a moving kinematic support as it does with `friction_mode="fixed_normal"`.

  Project the `friction_mode="coulomb_newton"` contact update of `SolverSteel` onto its friction cone. This prevents its finite root-search tolerance from applying a small tangential impulse at a frictionless contact and spinning up a sliding sphere.

  Reject `contact_regularization > 0` together with a `friction_mode` other than `"fixed_normal"` in `SolverSteel`. The coupled updates of those modes ignored the regularization; only articulated contacts on the immediate response under `"coulomb_newton"`, which keep the fixed-normal update, applied it.
- Add to `SolverSteel`: shared contact anchors (`contact_shared_anchor`, `contact_friction_shared_anchor`), which apply contact or friction rows at the witness-point midpoint as PhysX does; row high-water marks (`row_watermark`, read with `constraint_row_watermarks()`); the experimental matrix-free row schedules `row_schedule="contact_then_internal"` and `"phased"`; and the default-off `use_parallel_streams` and `double_buffer` options, which run the size groups' factorization and response kernels on streams of their own and clear the mass-matrix and Jacobian buffers of the previous step on a separate stream. With `use_parallel_streams`, groups of articulations with one shared joint topology assemble and factor their mass matrices in one kernel. None of these change the default solve.

  Skip the second row of each friction pair in `SolverSteel`'s fused matrix-free Gauss-Seidel sweeps, whose pair solve at the first row already writes it, and reuse the prefetched Jacobian for the pair's cross term, preserving the reduction order. No migration is required.

  Speed up `SolverSteel`'s fused matrix-free Gauss-Seidel sweeps, most for the `contact_then_internal` and `phased` row schedules and the propagation responses; results are bitwise unchanged.

Acting as a proxy destination raises `NotImplementedError` on the first coupled step.
