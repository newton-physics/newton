Add the `digital_instron_midsole` example that turns the calibrated `projects/digital_instron_v2` column model into a fully dynamic viscoelastic midsole coupled into Newton rigid-body physics. It runs four scenarios that share one live Hyperfoam-Maxwell-Pasternak foundation with anchored stick-slip (elastoplastic Coulomb) friction, accumulating the full six-component ground-reaction wrench into `newton.State.body_f`:

  - `--mode instron`: a displacement-controlled digital Instron that squishes the midsole between a shoe-last crosshead and the ground plane and records the force-displacement hysteresis loop.
  - `--mode settle`: a free, massive midsole that rests in stable equilibrium on the foundation and holds a sub-cone lateral load through the anchored stick-slip foam friction.
  - `--mode stride`: a synthetic running stride that rolls a foot heel-to-toe over the foundation, producing a ground-reaction force profile and a migrating center of pressure.
  - `--mode attached`: a fully dynamic, foot-mounted shoe with mass and inertia, held to the foot by a damped bilateral upper so the midsole presses the foam into the ground in stance and the whole bed lifts clear with the foot in flight. Its substep loop runs fully on the GPU (precomputed periodic foot trajectory, force resets fused into one kernel launch) and is captured into a single CUDA graph replayed per frame, running about 17x faster than eager launches (a few ms/frame, several times faster than real time); pass `--eager` to disable capture.


Add a differentiable variant of the column-bed foundation for gradient-based calibration and design. `projects/digital_instron_v2/dynamics_diff.py` exposes `DifferentiableMidsoleFoundation`, an autodiff-ready force model whose whole per-substep integration can be recorded on a `warp.Tape`: the generalized-Maxwell overstress recurrence is unrolled with per-substep history (no in-place aliasing) and the anchored stick-slip friction is replaced by a smooth, cone-respecting Coulomb law built on `warp.smooth_normalize`, while the equilibrium modulus, Hyperfoam exponent, overstress ratio, and Pasternak coupling are held in a `requires_grad` material vector. `projects/digital_instron_v2/inverse_id.py` uses it for `fit_material_to_force_curve`, a gradient-based foam material identification that differentiates a full digital-Instron load/unload cycle and recovers the material from a measured force-displacement curve (run `python -m projects.digital_instron_v2.inverse_id` for a self-identification demo). Gradients w.r.t. the initial state, the foam material, and the smooth friction are validated against finite differences in `newton/tests/test_digital_instron_diff.py`.

Extend the differentiable foam calibration to the measured Instron trials. `projects/digital_instron_v2/inverse_id.py` adds `DifferentiableTrial`, an autodiff sibling of `projects.digital_instron_v2.core.predict` that reads the per-column strain history of a shaped-indenter trial (the spherical rearfoot punch or the full-foot shoe last) and reproduces its quasi-static force-displacement curve to machine precision by evaluating the periodic generalized-Maxwell overstress fixed point on a `warp.Tape`. `fit_material_to_trials` uses it for a peak-normalized joint fit that recovers the shipped scipy calibration from the measured force curves with exact gradients, and shows the lower residual each fixture reaches on its own (run `python -m projects.digital_instron_v2.inverse_id` for the measured-trial demo).

Extend the differentiable midsole to the dynamic gait scenarios in `projects/digital_instron_v2/scenarios_diff.py`. `DifferentiableStride` rolls the foam bed through a kinematic heel-to-toe stride and reproduces the shipped `MidsoleFoundation` ground-reaction force exactly, and `DifferentiableAttached` drives a fully dynamic foot-mounted shoe (mass and inertia, a damped PD upper, and `newton.solvers.SolverSemiImplicit`) so the ground-reaction force can be differentiated with respect to the foam material through the whole rigid-body loop -- contact, PD actuation, and smooth Coulomb friction -- which the quasi-static `core.predict` cannot do. Both drivers accumulate the per-substep force with a write-once kernel and use a counter-free PD kernel so nothing on the loss path is aliased across substeps; the gradient is exact while contact is continuous and a valid subgradient across contact make/break during the roll (run `python -m projects.digital_instron_v2.scenarios_diff` for the stride and attached demo).

Make the Coulomb friction coefficient differentiable for friction identification. `DifferentiableMidsoleFoundation` now holds `mu` in a separate `requires_grad` `friction_params` array (distinct from the constitutive `material_params`), so a lateral- or shear-force objective can be differentiated with respect to friction as well as the foam material. Both dynamic drivers record the per-substep net lateral shear on the tape, and a new `DifferentiableSlide` driver -- a kinematic constant-velocity lateral drag at fixed penetration -- provides the clean continuous-contact setting in which the drag impulse is a smooth, monotone function of `mu`, so the coefficient can be recovered from a measured lateral force by gradient descent (exact in one step for the smooth-friction law).
