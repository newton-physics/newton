Bases: SolverBase

Reduced-coordinate articulated dynamics with a projected Gauss-Seidel constraint solve.

Experimental

SolverSteel, its constructor options and its solver-specific attributes may change without the normal deprecation period.

Each step evaluates forward kinematics and inverse dynamics on the articulation tree, builds the joint-space mass matrix with the composite rigid body algorithm (CRBA) and factors it, and integrates an unconstrained velocity prediction (Featherstone, Rigid Body Dynamics Algorithms, Springer, 2014). Contacts, joint position limits and joint velocity limits then become constraint rows solved by projected Gauss-Seidel (PGS) in impulse space. Rows of articulated bodies use the response Y = H^-1 J^T; contacts between free rigid bodies (a single body with a world-rooted free joint) and the ground or other free bodies use per-body inverse inertia. Positions are integrated from the solved velocities with symplectic Euler.

solve_mode selects the solve:

  • "matrix_free" (default, CUDA only): for each row the solver keeps Y and the diagonal J Y and recomputes J v every iteration instead of assembling a Delassus matrix. Every row applies its impulse to one world-local velocity vector immediately, and one fused kernel sweeps all rows of a world. Each feature below is available in it; some combinations are rejected, as the options document.

  • "split" (CPU and CUDA; required on CPU): the rows of articulated bodies of each world are assembled into a dense Delassus matrix C = J H^-1 J^T (articulation_constraint_max squared per world) and solved in impulse space; the free-body rows are then solved against the resulting velocity. Worlds with both kinds of rows alternate one sweep of each per iteration. It implements the base feature set and raises NotImplementedError when any of these is active: joint velocity limits, PGS drive rows, mimic and loop-closing joints, friction patches, contact regularization, restitution, warm start, velocity-only iterations, contact torsion, contact compliance, sleeping and the propagation contact responses. Contact torsion is CUDA-only, so on a CPU device a positive torsion radius raises ValueError first.

articulated_contact_response selects how contacts of articulated bodies are solved. The default "immediate" response uses the generalized rows above. The "propagation" responses instead solve them as fixed-size rows of the touched bodies with each body’s response from the articulated-body factorization of its tree, and propagate the impulses of each iteration through the tree to the joint velocities; "propagation_fused" runs the whole solve in one kernel launch and "propagation_colored" solves the contacts of each world in parallel batches.

Like SolverFeatherstone, the solver uses joint_q and joint_qd as its state and does not simulate bodies that are not connected through joints. Floating bases and free bodies need an explicit free joint, see add_joint_free() (add_body() adds one). Free-joint joint_qd is (v_com, omega) in the frame of the joint’s parent anchor (joint_X_p), which is the world frame for an identity anchor, the convention of newton.eval_fk().

Supported features:

  • Joints: PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, D6 and root FREE / DISTANCE joints.

  • Joint drives: joint_target_ke and joint_target_kd on PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE and D6 DOFs. By default (drive_mode="augmented") they are integrated implicitly by folding dt * kd + dt^2 * ke into the mass matrix; only the explicit drive force is clamped to joint_effort_limit, and the implicit stiffness and damping response is unbounded, so under a large external load the drive reaction can exceed the limit. drive_mode="rows" (solve_mode="matrix_free" only) instead solves one PGS row per driven DOF with the PhysX articulation force-drive update and clamps its impulse, which bounds the complete reaction. joint_armature and joint_damping are applied.

  • Joint limits: with enable_joint_limits=True, every finite joint_limit_lower / joint_limit_upper of a PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE or D6 DOF is a unilateral row. Joint limits are not enforced by default. Joint velocity limits (joint_velocity_limit) are enforced as rows when enable_joint_velocity_limits is set.

  • Mimic joints (set_joint_mimic(), and the deprecated mimic constraints) within one articulation: one bilateral row per follower coordinate enforces q_follower = coeffs[0] + coeffs[1] * q_leader. The coefficients are read every step.

  • Loop-closing BALL joints (joints outside the articulation tree whose child belongs to an articulation): three bilateral rows pin the joint’s parent and child anchors together. The parent is in the child’s articulation, a kinematic body, or the world. The closure rows read joint_enabled and the translations of joint_X_p / joint_X_c every step, so a closure is released, engaged or re-anchored by editing those arrays in place, also from a kernel between CUDA graph replays. An eager step reads a replaced array; a captured graph keeps the arrays it was captured with, so recapture after replacing one. With enable_bilateral_preelimination the mimic and connect rows are eliminated before the sweep through a regularized Schur complement; this is not exact elimination, see the option.

  • Contacts: rigid contacts from CollisionPipeline, one normal row per contact, with Coulomb friction from the mean of the two shapes’ mu. By default friction acts through persistent friction patches: compatible contacts of a body pair form a region whose friction is applied at up to two anchors that share the region’s normal load and carry their tangential displacement across steps, so static friction holds without creep (friction_anchor_beta). Setting friction_anchor_beta=0 selects point friction, two coupled tangent rows per contact. Restitution follows the shapes’ restitution. Optional contact regularization, warm start from the previous step’s impulses, velocity-only iterations and gap gates for speculative contacts are configured on the constructor. Experimental contact torsion (per-shape steel:min_torsional_patch_radius) adds a load-bounded spin-friction row per contact group, and experimental implicit compliance of hydroelastic contacts is opt-in (contact_compliance). Friction patches, restitution, regularization, warm start, velocity-only iterations, contact torsion and contact compliance need solve_mode="matrix_free"; the split solve uses point friction.

  • Kinematic bodies (KINEMATIC) and heterogeneous worlds.

  • CUDA graph capture of step() and reset().

  • Experimental passive-island sleeping (enable_sleeping, solve_mode="matrix_free" only): settled, supported islands of articulations freeze their published state and skip their rows and dynamics until a wake event.

Limitations:

  • solve_mode="matrix_free", the default, requires a CUDA device; constructing it on a CPU device raises NotImplementedError that names solve_mode="split".

  • Mimic relationships across articulations or on joints whose position and velocity coordinates differ (BALL, FREE, DISTANCE), loop-closing joints of other types, and loop closures between two dynamic articulations raise NotImplementedError. With solve_mode="split", every mimic relationship and loop-closing joint raises NotImplementedError; they need solve_mode="matrix_free". joint_enabled is supported only for loop-closing joints: construction raises for a disabled tree joint, and a tree joint disabled later is reported by check_constraint_capacity(). Enabled MuJoCo equality constraints (model.mujoco.equality_constraint_*) are enforced through the loop joint or mimic constraint the importer converted them to; an enabled row without such a conversion raises NotImplementedError, also when it is enabled later and notify_model_changed() is called with CONSTRAINT_PROPERTIES. A row counts as converted only when its target_kind / target link names a loop joint or mimic constraint between the row’s own bodies or joints. Particles are not simulated.

  • Gradients are not supported.

With solve_mode="matrix_free", branched articulations use sparse mass factors when every articulated (non-free-body) response group shares one joint topology with at most 64 DOFs, joint velocity-limit rows are disabled, the model has no mimic or loop-closing joints, drives are augmented and contacts use hard point friction (friction_anchor_beta=0, no warm start, regularization, velocity-only iterations, friction gap threshold, restitution, contact torsion or compliance): the mass matrix is assembled and factored in the fill-free pattern of the kinematic tree, and constraint rows keep only the DOFs that support them. Otherwise, and for free bodies, dense factors are used. Both give the same dynamics up to floating-point rounding. The selection follows the model’s structure only; it is not a performance prediction. Sparse factors take longer to set up and, depending on the articulation and the number of worlds, can run faster or markedly slower than dense factors. Articulations whose DOFs are not coupled at all, for example independent single-DOF branches of a fixed base, have a diagonal mass matrix; they skip the factorization and solve each DOF by division, bitwise identical to the dense loop kernels and equal to the tiled kernels up to float32 rounding, and take precedence over sparse factors. parallel_tree=True additionally traverses the independent branches of each tree in parallel.

Constraint rows are stored per world with fixed capacities (articulation_constraint_max for rows of articulated bodies, free_body_constraint_max for free-body contacts, and a propagation-row capacity derived from both, see articulated_contact_response). Rows that do not fit are dropped and the world is flagged in constraint_overflow, a device boolean array with one entry per world plus a final entry for global (world -1) articulations. It also records contact-buffer overflow and contacts dropped by global contact reduction. The flags persist until reset(). Read them in a device kernel (for example an RL termination term) or call check_constraint_capacity() outside graph capture.

Global articulations are solved together with world 0. A kinematic global free body (for example a moving platform) contacts the bodies of every world.

Known limitation: a contact between an articulated body of another world and a dynamic global body, or a kinematic global articulation with joints, cannot be solved, because it would couple two worlds’ solves. Such a contact is dropped, counted, reported once by a device-side warning (unless warn_constraint_overflow is off) and flagged in constraint_overflow. The constructor warns once if the model’s shape flags and collision groups allow such contacts. Use world geometry or a kinematic free body for scenery shared by all worlds, or add the body to every world.

Solver observables:

body_parent_f is populated when BODY_PARENT_F is requested from observables(). As in SolverFeatherstone, it is the per-body net spatial wrench of the inverse-dynamics backward pass at the start of the step, translated to the body’s center of mass (linear force [N] first, torque [N·m] second, world frame). It does not include constraint or contact impulses of the step. With sleeping enabled the solver computes the wrench every step into its own history, so a sleeping body reports its last awake wrench in whichever output the caller passes.

contact_f is populated when CONTACT_F is requested. Each live rigid contact row holds the force [N] on shape 0’s body, in world frame, from the solved normal and friction impulses of the step divided by dt; the torque part is zero (torsional friction is not reported). Contacts whose rows were dropped for capacity, and contacts of sleeping islands whose rows are skipped (sleep_skip_constraints), report zero, as do rows beyond the contact count or the rigid capacity and the soft-contact rows. A zero force under a sleeping body does not mean the contact was lost: force-threshold logic and SensorContact see an apparent unloading while the island sleeps; pass sleep_skip_constraints=False (or leave sleeping off) to keep the solved forces.

Example

solver = newton.solvers.SolverSteel(model)
pipeline = newton.CollisionPipeline(model)
contacts = pipeline.contacts()

for i in range(100):
    pipeline.collide(state_in, contacts)
    solver.step(state_in, state_out, control, contacts, dt)
    state_in, state_out = state_out, state_in
classmethod register_custom_attributes(builder)#

Register the body and shape attributes consumed by Steel in the steel namespace.

Each attribute maps to the matching PhysX rigid-body USD attribute:

  • steel:rigid_body_angular_damping [1/s] damps the angular velocity of free bodies and floating bases as w *= 1 - d dt each step (physxRigidBody:angularDamping, default 0.05, as SolverFeatherstone); joints with a parent body are not damped. Fill the attribute for one value on every body.

  • steel:rigid_body_max_linear_velocity [m/s] and steel:rigid_body_max_angular_velocity [rad/s] bound the velocity of free bodies (physxRigidBody:maxLinearVelocity and physxRigidBody:maxAngularVelocity, default unbounded).

  • steel:rigid_body_max_depenetration_velocity [m/s] bounds the contact correction velocity of free bodies (physxRigidBody:maxDepenetrationVelocity, default unbounded).

One per-shape attribute enables contact torsion, see contact_torsion_device:

  • steel:min_torsional_patch_radius [m] is the effective spin radius of the shape’s contacts (physxCollision:minTorsionalPatchRadius, default 0, no torsion). PhysX also scales a radius with the penetration depth through physxCollision:torsionalPatchRadius; this solver does not.

The solver reads them from the model at every step, for example model.steel.rigid_body_angular_damping. Models built without these attributes use the defaults.

Parameters:

builder (ModelBuilder) – Model builder to register the attributes with.

Create a Steel solver for a finalized model.

The options below configure this solver instance at runtime; they are not model or USD attributes. Per-body physical parameters are model attributes, see register_custom_attributes().

Parameters:

  • model (Model) – Model to simulate.

  • solve_mode (Literal['matrix_free', 'split']) – Constraint solve. "matrix_free" (default, CUDA only) sweeps every row in one fused kernel and recomputes J v from the current velocity. "split" (CPU and CUDA; pass it explicitly on CPU) assembles the rows of articulated bodies into a dense Delassus matrix J H^-1 J^T per world, solves it in impulse space and then solves the free-body rows against the resulting velocity; worlds that contain both kinds of rows alternate one sweep of each per iteration. The split solve raises for the matrix-free-only options listed in the class description.

  • iterations (int) – Number of projected Gauss-Seidel iterations per step.

  • baumgarte (float) – Baumgarte position-correction factor of contact and joint-limit rows, as a fraction of the position error removed per step.

  • cfm (float) – Constraint force mixing added to every row’s effective-mass diagonal, which regularizes redundant rows. Drive rows of drive_mode="rows" use their exact unit response instead.

  • relaxation (float) – Successive over-relaxation factor of the PGS sweep.

  • update_mass_matrix_interval (int) – Rebuild and refactor the mass matrix every this many calls to step(); 1 rebuilds it every step. Larger values reuse a stale factorization between rebuilds. A change of dt, reset() and notify_model_changed() still request a refresh of the affected articulations. Under CUDA graph capture the host-side cadence is baked into the graph, so the interval should divide the number of steps captured per graph.

  • enable_joint_limits (bool) – Enforce joint_limit_lower and joint_limit_upper of PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE and D6 DOFs as unilateral rows. When False (the default), no joint-limit rows are created, joint position limits are not enforced, and limits use no articulation_constraint_max capacity. The default matches the Steel reference implementation this solver is ported from.

  • joint_limit_activation_gap (float) – Distance from a finite position limit [m or rad] at which its row is created, when enable_joint_limits is set. inf creates the rows of every finite limit each step; a smaller gap creates a row only when q <= lower + gap or q >= upper - gap. Every finite bound is enforced, however large: the builder’s default limits (+/-1e10) are finite, so with inf each such DOF uses two rows of articulation_constraint_max in every step. Use a finite gap or unbounded (+/-inf) limits to avoid these rows; the constructor warns when the finite limits alone exceed the capacity. The value is validated even when joint limits are disabled.

  • enable_joint_velocity_limits (bool) – Enforce joint_velocity_limit of PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE and D6 DOFs with one row pair per limited DOF, after scaling articulation velocities that already exceed a limit. Requires solve_mode="matrix_free". The free-body limits of register_custom_attributes() are enforced whenever those attributes are registered, independently of this option and of solve_mode.

  • velocity_limit_activation_fraction (float) – Create the velocity-limit rows of a DOF only when |qd| >= velocity_limit_activation_fraction * limit. 0 creates them for every limited DOF each step; inf never creates them. The gate samples the velocity before the solve, so a DOF that crosses the threshold during a step is clamped one step later. Must be in [0, 1] or inf.

  • articulation_constraint_max (int) – Capacity of rows involving articulated bodies (mimic and connect rows, contacts, enabled joint limits, joint velocity limits and, with drive_mode="rows", joint drives) per world. Rows beyond it are dropped and reported, see constraint_overflow. The split solve also stores a articulation_constraint_max x articulation_constraint_max Delassus matrix per world.

  • free_body_constraint_max (int) – Capacity of free-body contact rows per world. Rows beyond it are dropped and reported, see constraint_overflow.

  • warn_constraint_overflow (bool) – Print a device-side warning the first time a world exceeds a row capacity. The warning does not synchronize the host and is compatible with CUDA graph capture.

  • row_watermark (bool) – Accumulate the high-water marks of the per-world row counts of every row family and of the contact count on the device in every step, including captured ones; read them with constraint_row_watermarks().

  • drive_mode (Literal['augmented', 'rows']) – Joint drive formulation. "augmented" integrates drives implicitly in the mass matrix. "rows" (solve_mode="matrix_free" only) adds one dense PGS row per driven DOF (positive joint_target_ke or joint_target_kd) whose impulse follows the PhysX articulation force-drive update in every iteration, so drives are solved together with contacts and limits instead of before them. Without contacts or limits and below the effort limit, the converged row reproduces the implicit drive of "augmented". Under effort saturation the two differ: "augmented" clamps the explicit drive force before the implicit solve, while the row clamps its accumulated impulse at joint_effort_limit * dt. Drive rows are allocated first in each world, count against articulation_constraint_max, and a drive whose row does not fit is not applied (the world is flagged in constraint_overflow).

  • fuse_joint_velocity_limits (bool) – With drive_mode="rows" and enable_joint_velocity_limits, enforce the velocity limit of each driven DOF with a clamp of its velocity at the end of every iteration (after the contact rows, like the velocity-limit rows it replaces) instead of two velocity-limit rows, and exclude it from the pre-solve velocity scaling. Limited DOFs without a drive row keep their rows. In every other configuration, including velocity_limit_activation_fraction=inf, the option has no effect; velocity_limits_fused reports whether the clamp is active.

  • enable_bilateral_preelimination (bool) – Eliminate the mimic and connect rows of each articulation before the iterative sweep with a Schur complement of the bilateral block S = J_B H^-1 J_B^T: the predicted velocity is projected once and the responses of the other rows are corrected, so closures and mimic couplings depend much less on iterations. The block is factored with a diagonal regularization R of 1e-3 times each row’s diagonal plus a floor of max(cfm, 1e-7), which keeps nearly dependent closure axes positive definite. The elimination is therefore not exact: a bilateral velocity residual of R (S + R)^-1 (J_B v + b_B) remains after the projection, and the corrected responses of other rows still couple into the bilateral rows by the same factor. The rows stay allocated and in the sweep, which reduces this residual further. If any articulation owns more than eight bilateral rows, any loop closure has a kinematic or world parent, or velocity_iterations is positive, elimination is disabled for the whole solver (every articulation keeps iterative rows) with a warning at construction.

  • bilateral_preelimination_include_mimics (bool) – With pre-elimination enabled, also eliminate mimic rows. False eliminates only the connect rows and keeps mimic rows iterative, which avoids a singular block when a mimic row is nearly dependent on a closure.

  • parallel_tree (bool) – Traverse the independent branches of each articulation tree in parallel (forward kinematics and dynamics, the inverse-dynamics backward pass and state publication), with up to 32 threads per articulation instead of one. Results match the serial traversal up to floating-point summation order. Unbranched articulations, models whose joints are not ordered parent before child, and CPU devices keep the serial traversal. Broad trees can benefit; narrow trees can be slower, so measure the full step before enabling it.

  • use_parallel_streams (bool) – Run the factorization, unconstrained solve and response kernels of each articulation-size group on a CUDA stream of its own, so groups of different sizes overlap. Results are unchanged; CPU devices and models with one size group ignore it.

  • double_buffer (bool) – Keep two sets of the grouped mass-matrix and Jacobian buffers and clear the set a step used on a separate CUDA stream while the next step runs on the other set. Results are unchanged; CPU devices and the sparse mass factors ignore it. Within a captured graph the clearing overlaps the next step; after an eager step, the stream’s later work waits for its clearing. Steps and graph launches of one solver must be ordered on one stream (or through explicit stream dependencies).

  • friction_anchor_beta (float | None) – Position-correction gain of persistent friction patches; 0 selects point friction. None selects 0.2 with solve_mode="matrix_free", and point friction with solve_mode="split", which has no friction patches, or when contact_compliance is enabled. With a positive gain, the contacts of a body pair whose normals, contact planes and friction coefficients agree and whose shapes touch form a region. Each region’s friction acts at up to two anchors along its principal extent, which share the region’s total normal impulse; every contact keeps its own normal row. An anchor carries its tangential displacement across steps, following the pose increments of both bodies, and the tangent rows add friction_anchor_beta * displacement / dt, so a held object does not creep under a static load. Anchors are released when their region is unloaded or slides at the Coulomb limit; history is matched by body pair and geometry, independently of collision contact matching. A one-anchor region has no torsional friction of its own. Call reset() after teleporting a body to discard its history.

  • contact_regularization (float) – Dimensionless regularization g of penetrating contact rows (solve_mode="matrix_free" only). Each iteration moves a row’s impulse toward the rigid solution with weight 1 / (1 + g) and toward zero with weight g / (1 + g), the update of an implicitly integrated contact spring. It makes statically indeterminate normal-force splits unique and damps the sweep, at the cost of a resting penetration: a row at rest settles at baumgarte * phi / dt = -g * d * lambda (d its unsplit inverse effective mass, lambda its impulse), about g * a * dt^2 / baumgarte for a body under acceleration a resting on one row. Propagation rows whose response is split across coupled contact bodies, with split diagonal d_s, take weight w * d_s / (w * d_s + (1 - w) * d) with w = 1 / (1 + g), which reaches the same rest state. Speculative (positive-gap) rows, rows whose rebound fires and the velocity-only iterations stay rigid. 0 is the rigid law; at most 1e6.

  • velocity_iterations (int) – Number of velocity-only iterations after the position solve (solve_mode="matrix_free" only). Positions are integrated from the position solve; these iterations then refine the velocity without the position bias, so stabilization does not add velocity to the bodies. A positive-gap contact whose linearized gap at the end of the step stays open keeps its speculative allowance, and an impacting contact keeps its rebound target.

  • velocity_drive_mode (Literal['freeze', 'active']) – Treatment of PGS joint-drive rows during the velocity-only iterations: "freeze" keeps the drive impulses of the position solve, "active" keeps solving the drive rows. Implicit (mass-matrix) drives have no rows, so the option has no effect for them.

  • friction_mode (Literal['fixed_normal', 'bisection', 'bisection_desaxce', 'coulomb_newton']) – Experimental Coulomb friction update of the matrix-free solve (solve_mode="matrix_free" only). "fixed_normal" updates both tangent impulses together on the friction disk of the current normal impulse: sticking uses the inverse of their 2x2 block, sliding a bounded scalar solve that keeps isotropic maximum dissipation. "bisection" bisects the normal impulse of each contact and re-solves the 2x2 tangent problem at every probe, so normal and friction impulses are updated together. "bisection_desaxce" adds the de Saxce maximum-dissipation bias mu * |c_T| to the normal target velocity of free-body contacts (Le Lidec and Carpentier, 2024). "coulomb_newton" solves each free-body contact’s Coulomb cone exactly with a bracketed scalar Newton iteration on the tangential-force ratio; articulated contacts keep the "fixed_normal" update. The other modes use point friction: an omitted friction_anchor_beta selects it with a warning, and friction patches, contact torsion, contact compliance and the propagation responses require "fixed_normal". This option may change without the normal deprecation period.

  • row_schedule (Literal['interleaved', 'contact_then_internal', 'phased']) – Row order of the matrix-free sweeps (solve_mode="matrix_free" only). "interleaved" sweeps every row family in each iteration: the dense rows, the free-body contact rows, then the velocity limits. "contact_then_internal" runs every iteration of the contact rows first, then every iteration of the internal rows (drive, mimic, connect and joint-limit rows) and the velocity limits. "phased" approximates the order PhysX uses for grasping: each iteration solves the internal rows, then the contact rows, then the velocity limits, each in a launch of its own; the propagation responses use this order with "interleaved" as well. Contact torsion and contact compliance require "interleaved", "propagation_fused" rejects "contact_then_internal", and the sparse mass factors are selected only with "interleaved".

  • contact_warmstart (bool) – Start each step (solve_mode="matrix_free" only) from the previous step’s contact impulses, matched by contact identity through rigid_contact_match_index, so the CollisionPipeline must be created with contact matching enabled. Substeps that reuse a contact set without a collision pass seed each contact from its own previous substep. History carries across one collision pass into the same Contacts buffer when that pass matched against the solved contact set (rigid_contact_match_generation); a different buffer, skipped passes, or a pass after the pipeline wrote another buffer start cold. Carried impulses are scaled by the ratio of the step to the previous one, including under graph replay; tangent impulses are rotated into the current tangent frame and clamped to the current friction cone; other rows start cold.

  • contact_warmstart_decay (float) – Non-negative scale applied to the carried impulses.

  • enable_restitution (bool) – Apply contact restitution, the only restitution switch. The default True leaves the shapes’ coefficients in charge; they default to zero, so a model that authors no restitution does not bounce. False turns restitution off for every contact regardless of the coefficients, and lets the split solve and contact_compliance accept shapes with positive restitution.

  • restitution_velocity_threshold (float) – Minimum incident normal speed [m/s] for a rebound. Contacts bounce with the arithmetic mean of the two shapes’ restitution coefficients (restitution, clamped to [0, 1]; zero gives no rebound): an impacting contact replaces its position bias by the rebound target -e * u, where u is its incident normal velocity in the unconstrained prediction. The contact must also touch, or be predicted to reach the surface during the step. Slower contacts keep the ordinary contact law, so resting contacts do not bounce under small accelerations. Finite and non-negative. Restitution needs solve_mode="matrix_free"; the split solve rejects shapes with a positive restitution unless enable_restitution=False.

  • contact_speculative_scale (float) – Fraction of a positive contact gap that the contact may close during the step (rhs = scale * phi / dt). 0 removes the speculative allowance; penetration correction is unchanged.

  • contact_gap_gate (float | None) – Contacts of the collision pipeline whose gap [m] exceeds this distance get no rows; 0 keeps touching contacts only. None (the default) keeps every contact. gap decides which contacts the pipeline reports (also to sensors and other solvers); this gate only decides which of them this solver solves. Finite and non-negative.

  • contact_friction_scale (float) – Non-negative factor applied to every shape’s friction coefficient (shape_material_mu) in the solver’s copy of the materials, refreshed at construction and by notify_model_changed() with SHAPE_PROPERTIES. It scales the friction rows, patches and the torsion budget; the model’s coefficients are unchanged.

  • contact_friction_position_iterations (int) – Number of position iterations that solve tangential friction rows. -1 (the default) solves them in every position iteration; k >= 0 solves them only in the last k position iterations (0 never), and holds their impulses at zero before that. Velocity-only iterations always solve friction. Values other than -1 reject contact_warmstart and contact_compliance.

  • contact_friction_gap_max (float | None) – Contacts with a gap [m] above this distance get a normal row only. None (the default) gives every contact friction rows; -inf gives none of them friction rows, which saves the rows that zero friction coefficients would keep. A finite negative value keeps friction rows for contacts that penetrate deeper than it. Switching between None (or inf) and a finite or -inf value requires a new solver.

  • contact_shared_anchor (bool) – Apply every contact row at the midpoint of the two witness points instead of at each body’s own witness point, as PhysX applies a contact at one point. phi still comes from the witness points. Friction patch rows keep their patch anchors, so with patches this moves only the normal rows.

  • contact_friction_shared_anchor (bool) – Apply the friction rows at the witness midpoint, which removes the tangential force couple of witness points separated along the normal. It has no effect on friction patch rows, which act at their anchors.

  • contact_torsion_device (bool) – Experimental contact torsion is enabled at construction when any shape has a positive steel:min_torsional_patch_radius (see register_custom_attributes()). It adds one angular row per contact group of an articulated body, about the group’s normal, bounded by r * (mu * N - sliding): sliding and spin share one Coulomb budget, N is the final solved load of the group’s normal rows, and r is the largest radius of the group’s shapes (a patch region can span several shape pairs; contacts whose pair has no positive radius get no torsion). With point friction a group is a coplanar cluster of touching contacts of one shape pair; with friction patches it is one patch region, budgeted by its pooled load and undivided friction coefficient. The radius is an effective footprint, not derived from the geometry: for uniform pressure on a disk of radius R it is 2 * R / 3. Rows are rebuilt every step and carry no spin history; free-body contacts get none. Supported geometry is sphere, box, capsule, cylinder, cone, ellipsoid, plane and convex mesh. Requires contact_warmstart=False; contacts with hydroelastic stiffness are rejected, and running out of articulation_constraint_max raises instead of dropping rows. contact_torsion_device prepares the torsion rows on the device instead of on the host. The host preparation is the reference implementation; it reads the contacts back every step and cannot be captured in a CUDA graph. The device preparation builds the same groups and rows with preallocated buffers and supports CUDA graph capture of step(). Invalid torsion input (for example rows beyond the capacity or hydroelastic contacts) makes an eager step raise before it applies torsion rows. In a captured step the error is deferred: the failing step restores its output motion arrays (joint_q, joint_qd, body_q, body_qd, body_qdd) to the input values, while other outputs, observables and solver history still advance, and check_constraint_capacity() raises after the replays. Check before using replay results, discard failed output, fix the cause and reset(). The error is solver-wide: any reset clears it, and a cause that persists latches it again on the next step. Grouping runs serially per world with worst-case quadratic cost in the contacts of a world. No effect without contact torsion.

  • contact_compliance (bool) – Experimental: solve contacts that carry a positive rigid_contact_stiffness [N/m] with an implicit unilateral spring-damper law instead of the rigid normal law. The material comes from the contacts (per-shape hydroelastic stiffness, the contact damping [N s/m], zero staying zero, and the friction weight, applied once to the pair friction); contacts with zero stiffness stay rigid. A compliant row’s force is max(0, -k phi_next - c u_next), solved by updating its residual u + k phi / (dt k + c) + lambda / (dt (dt k + c)) after every sweep; the damping term is dropped while the gap is open. Requires point friction (None or 0 for friction_anchor_beta; None warns), positive iterations, no warm start, no velocity-only iterations, no contact regularization, no shared anchors and zero shape restitution. The contacts must carry the hydroelastic material arrays. Each step synchronizes the row metadata with the host, so the option rejects CUDA graph capture and is not a performance path. Contacts the allocator excludes on purpose are counted in compliance_skipped_contact_count and compliant contacts in compliance_contact_count; a compliant contact lost to a row capacity raises, even with warn_constraint_overflow off. This option may change without the normal deprecation period.

  • enable_sleeping (bool) – Experimental passive-island sleeping. Articulations in contact are joined into islands every step. Static geometry and articulations with no responding degrees of freedom (such as a body welded to the world) support an island without joining it. A fixed-base articulation with responding joints, such as a robot arm, joins the island of everything it touches, so it can connect them into one island; its fixed root supports that island. A supported island whose bodies stay below sleep_linear_threshold and sleep_angular_threshold for sleep_quiet_time falls asleep, and its published state (joint_q, joint_qd, body_q, body_qd and, when requested, body_parent_f) is frozen until a wake event: a nonzero or nonfinite external force (body_f, joint_f), a changed state or gravity, a contact with an awake or moving body, lost support or row capacity, a reset() of its world, or notify_model_changed() for properties of its joints, DOFs, bodies or shapes (other notifications and changes to static or kinematic owners wake every island). Driven articulations (nonzero drive gains) stay awake. Sleeping is configured at construction; rebuild captured graphs to change it. Construction raises ValueError with contact_warmstart or velocity_iterations, or for a model without articulations; step() raises ValueError for a nonpositive or nonfinite dt and when state_in and state_out share arrays. The sleeping options are runtime solver settings with no USD schema.

  • sleep_linear_threshold (float) – Body center-of-mass speed below which a body is quiet [m/s], finite and positive.

  • sleep_angular_threshold (float) – Body angular speed below which a body is quiet [rad/s], finite and positive.

  • sleep_quiet_time (float) – Quiet, supported interval after which an island sleeps [s], finite and positive.

  • sleep_skip_constraints (bool) – Skip the work of sleeping islands: their contact and joint-limit rows (collision still runs, so wake events are complete), the rebuild of their friction-patch history (carried instead), and their articulated dynamics (kinematics, inverse dynamics, drives, mass matrix and factorization, velocity prediction and integration). False keeps the rows and dynamics of sleeping islands and only freezes their published state.

  • articulated_contact_response (Literal['immediate', 'propagation', 'propagation_fused', 'propagation_colored']) –

    How contacts touching an articulation that is not a free body are solved.

    • "immediate": dense rows of the articulation’s generalized coordinates, Y = H^-1 J^T per row.

    • "propagation": fixed-size rows of the touched bodies at their centers of mass, with each body’s 6x6 response from the articulated-body factorization of its tree. Each iteration solves these rows and then propagates the accumulated body impulses through the trees to the joint velocities. All contacts, including those of free bodies, use these rows, whose capacity per world is free_body_constraint_max + articulation_constraint_max.

    • "propagation_fused": the same response, with every iteration of the whole solve in one kernel launch. Free-body contacts keep their free-body rows; the propagation row capacity per world is articulation_constraint_max. Requires every non-free articulation that responds to have the same number of DOFs.

    • "propagation_colored": the "propagation" rows and capacity, with each sweep solving the contacts of a world in batches of contacts that share no responding body, one batch after the other. The batches are recolored every step; contacts left without a batch run last in contact order.

    The propagation responses avoid the per-row H^-1 J^T of contact rows at the cost of tree passes over every articulation in each iteration; which response is faster depends on the model. They keep joint-limit and velocity-limit rows dense, and contacts between two links of the same articulation dense unless propagation_same_articulation_rows is set. The tree factorization is rebuilt every step, independently of update_mass_matrix_interval.

  • propagation_same_articulation_rows (bool) – Solve contacts between two links of the same articulation as propagation rows, with the exact cross response of the two links, instead of dense rows. Requires articulated_contact_response="propagation".

  • propagation_cached_response (bool) – With articulated_contact_response="propagation" or "propagation_colored", replace the per-iteration tree walk by cached responses: the setup extracts, for each contact-active body, its joint-space response R (DOFs x 6) and the 6x6 response blocks between active bodies, from the tree factorization that is fixed for the step, and each iteration applies them as a small matrix-vector product. The result equals the tree walk up to float reassociation. It applies to articulation groups with 0- or 1-DOF joints (optionally under a free root); other groups, and worlds with more cache-eligible active bodies than propagation_cached_response_body_max, keep the tree walk, selected per world on the device. Which is faster depends on the model.

  • propagation_cached_response_body_max (int) – Capacity of the cached responses in contact-active bodies of cache-eligible articulations per world; free bodies do not count. At least 1.

check_constraint_capacity()#

Raise if any world, or the global entry, lost contacts or constraint rows since its last reset.

Also raises if a step saw a joint of an articulation tree disabled in joint_enabled; only loop-closing joints can be disabled. That error is not a capacity loss: the flagged steps ignored the disabled joint, so discard their output, re-enable the joint and reset() the affected worlds. A check that passes after the reset does not validate the earlier output. With contact_torsion_device, also raises if contact torsion rejected its input since the last reset(); a failing captured step restored its output motion arrays to the input, while a failing eager step raised from step().

Reads the status on the host, so call it outside CUDA graph capture, for example at an observation boundary. Kernels can read constraint_overflow directly without host synchronization; it holds only the capacity flags. Recovering from a capacity loss requires larger capacities, a new solver (and newly captured graphs) and a reset() of the affected worlds. Contacts that couple a dynamic global body, or a kinematic global articulation with joints, with another world are flagged too; no capacity can solve them.

Raises:

RuntimeError – If called during graph capture, or if any entry is flagged.

constraint_row_watermarks()#

Return the row high-water marks accumulated since construction (row_watermark only).

Per row family (articulation, free_body, propagation): the largest retained and requested row count of any world and step, the largest number of dropped contact rows, the largest excess over the capacity, and the number of overflowing world-steps; and contact_high_water, the largest contact count. Reads the device on the host, so call it outside CUDA graph capture. Every value is 0 when row_watermark is off.

notify_model_changed(flags, *, world_mask=None)#

Refresh cached solver data after model changes.

Joint (frames), joint DOF, DOF force (drive gains), DOF inertial (armature), body (kinematic flags) and inertial changes request a mass-matrix refresh of every articulation on the next step, independent of update_mass_matrix_interval. Body flags (kinematic membership), armature, joint damping (joint DOF or DOF force changes) and shape friction and restitution coefficients are re-read. Other model data, such as gravity, limits and shape transforms, is read every step. A kinematic free body that was removed from the response at construction cannot become dynamic again; reconstruct the solver in that case. Constraint changes re-check the MuJoCo equality rows: enabling one that is not converted to a Newton loop joint or mimic constraint raises NotImplementedError. Capacity status in constraint_overflow is not cleared, see reset().

Body, inertial, joint DOF, DOF inertial and shape changes discard the warm-start impulses of the worlds selected by world_mask. With friction patches, shape changes also discard the anchors of the bodies whose collision geometry changed, in every world.

Joint, joint DOF (including the DOF inertial and force flags), body inertial and shape notifications run on the device without host synchronization, so they can be captured in a CUDA graph and replayed after the model arrays are edited in place. A captured notification reads the arrays it was captured with; after replacing a model array, notify eagerly or recapture. Body notifications read body_flags on the host. Sleeping, and the positive-restitution checks of the split solve and of sparse mass factors (with restitution enabled), also read model data on the host, so issue those notifications outside CUDA graph capture.

Parameters:

  • flags (ModelFlags | int) – Bit-mask of ModelFlags indicating which model properties changed.

  • world_mask (wp.array[wp.bool] | None) – Optional boolean mask of shape (world_count + 1,) in the reset() layout, selecting the worlds whose model data changed. Only their warm-start impulses are discarded; cached model data is refreshed for every world. None discards the warm-start impulses of every world.

reset(state, world_mask=None, flags=None)#

Clear solver-owned state of the selected worlds.

The simulation state is not modified. For the selected worlds this clears the capacity status in constraint_overflow, discards the warm-start impulses and the friction-patch anchors, and requests a mass-matrix refresh on the next step, so a teleported articulation does not reuse a stale factorization or anchor; other worlds keep their refresh cadence and history. An anchor between bodies of two entries (for example a global body and a world body) is discarded when either is selected. Global articulations share world 0’s rows, so selecting the global entry also discards world 0’s warm-start impulses while a dynamic global articulation exists. Any reset, whatever its mask, also clears the solver-wide contact torsion error; it clears the disabled-tree-joint status of the selected worlds. The reset is launched on the device without host synchronization and can be captured in a CUDA graph.

Parameters:

  • state (State) – Simulation state; left unchanged.

  • world_mask (wp.array[wp.bool] | None) – Optional boolean mask of shape (world_count + 1,). The first world_count entries select worlds; the final entry selects global articulations (world -1) and the final entry of constraint_overflow. None resets everything.

  • flags (StateFlags | int | None) – Unused; the solver’s history is cleared regardless of the state flags.

Advance the simulation by one time step.

  • state_out (State) – Output state receiving joint_q, joint_qd, body_q, body_qd and, when requested, body_parent_f.

  • contacts (Contacts | None) – Rigid contacts from CollisionPipeline, or None. The buffer must not hold more contacts than model.rigid_contact_max allowed when the solver was constructed.

  • dt (float) – Time step [s].

  • observables (Observables | None) – Optional solver observable arrays allocated by observables().

Returns:

state_out.
