Steel#

Steel is Skild AI’s rigid-body solver for articulated robots and rigid bodies. It is integrated into Newton as SolverSteel. The solver works in generalized coordinates, uses semi-implicit integration with implicit joint drives, and solves contacts, joint limits and the other constraint rows with a matrix-free projected Gauss-Seidel sweep that runs on CPU and CUDA.

Experimental

SolverSteel’s public API and behavior may change without prior notice. Its matrix-free solve runs on CPU and CUDA; contact torsion, contact compliance, joint friction, the non-default friction_mode values and sleeping with solve_mode="colored" require CUDA, and solve_mode="colored" runs the serial propagation sweep on CPU.

Within the Newton documentation, the SolverSteel API reference describes construction, supported features and the constructor options, the Steel rows of the Solver Tuning Reference cover its tuning knobs, and Deterministic Simulation covers its run-to-run determinism.

External resources#

Skild maintains the Steel documentation on its own site, and this page only points to it, so the two do not drift.

  • Steel documentation: the documentation site, with concepts, guides, reference pages and examples.

  • Quick start: a first Steel simulation, then many worlds under a CUDA graph.

  • Tuning: how to get a scene right and check a setting, when to add substeps or iterations, and recipes for common scenes.

  • Porting from PhysX: the guide for moving a PhysX or Isaac Lab scene to Steel. It covers attribute mapping, solver option equivalents, what differs, and a migration procedure.

  • The step pipeline: the two halves of a Steel step, the per-articulation dynamics and the per-world constraint solve.

  • Constructor options: every keyword of the SolverSteel constructor, generated from the solver’s source, with guidance on when to change it.

  • Examples: the example scenes, grouped by topic, each with the command that runs it.

  • skild-ai/steel on GitHub: the repository behind the documentation site, with runnable example scenes and step-by-step agent workflows for tuning, debugging, validating, and porting from PhysX.

  • Launch write-up: Skild’s announcement of Steel.
