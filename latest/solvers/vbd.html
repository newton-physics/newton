VBD#

SolverVBD is a unified implicit solver based on Vertex Block Descent (VBD): cloth, soft bodies, rigid bodies (joints, contacts, rods), and their interactions are all solved in one VBD loop, with joint and contact constraints enforced through an augmented-Lagrangian (ALM) extension of the method. The compliant ALM constraint path is enabled by default (rigid_compliant_alm=True). Pass rigid_compliant_alm=False explicitly to retain the deprecated legacy path during its migration period.

Experimental

SolverVBD is experimental. Its public API, behavior, feature support, performance, and implementation may change without prior notice.

This page is the VBD backend guide: when to choose the solver, how to set it up correctly, and where to find runnable examples. For symptom-driven diagnosis and parameter tuning, see VBD Tuning and the Simulation Tuning landing page.

When to Choose VBD#

Start from the Supported Features matrix and the Joint Feature Support tables in the solver overview. The underlying methods are described in the references cited by SolverVBD:

  • Anka He Chen, Ziheng Liu, Yin Yang, and Cem Yuksel. 2024. Vertex Block Descent. ACM Trans. Graph. 43, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/3658179

  • Chris Giles, Elie Diaz, and Cem Yuksel. 2025. Augmented Vertex Block Descent. ACM Trans. Graph. 44, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/3731195 (the deprecated legacy rigid constraint path)

  • Anka H. Chen, Jerry Hsu, Youssef Ayman, and Miles Macklin. 2026. Divide and Truncate: A Penetration and Inversion Free Framework for Coupled Multi-physics System. In ACM SIGGRAPH 2026 Conference Papers. https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811143 (penetration-free truncation, DAT)

  • Anka He Chen, Jerry Hsu, Ziheng Liu, Miles Macklin, Yin Yang, and Cem Yuksel. 2025. Offset Geometric Contact. ACM Trans. Graph. 44, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/3731205 (feature regions of full-surface mesh contact, OGC)

API Explanations#

Construction and Stepping#

Colour the model before you finalize it. color() fills particle_color_groups and body_color_groups; the solver raises at construction when particles are present without particle colours, or when rigid bodies are integrated by VBD without body colours. Pass include_bending=True when the model has bending edges. Then build the collision pipeline, its contacts buffer and the solver, in that order:

builder.color(include_bending=True)  # the model has bending edges
model = builder.finalize()

collision_pipeline = newton.CollisionPipeline(model)
contacts = collision_pipeline.contacts()
solver = newton.solvers.SolverVBD(model, iterations=10)

state_in, state_out = model.state(), model.state()
control = model.control()
for _ in range(num_steps):
    collision_pipeline.collide(state_in, contacts)
    solver.step(state_in, state_out, control, contacts, dt)
    state_in, state_out = state_out, state_in

For rigid joints the solver captures the structural rest pose from body_q at construction, so the body transforms must match the joint angles when the solver is built; ROD rest bend and twist come from joint_rest_q.

With a solver-owned pipeline, SolverVBD(model, collision_pipeline=...), the solver allocates its own contacts buffer, seeds the pipeline’s self-contact configuration from the particle_self_contact_* arguments, runs rigid detection itself on the schedule of collision_frequency_type, and step takes contacts=None. Rigid-soft DAT requires this form, and so does a RIGID slot on ITERATIONS, which also needs contact matching in the pipeline (below). The self-contact slot works in both forms; Jitter: where to look first shows the arguments that put it on the ITERATIONS schedule.

Contact matching between steps and between iterations. Each detection pass of the pipeline can label every body-body contact with the index of the same contact in the previous pass. contact_matching="latest" writes the labels and keeps the fresh geometry; "sticky" also replays the saved contact points and normal (experimental); the default "disabled" writes no labels (Contact Matching). The solver uses the labels in two places. Between steps, rigid_contact_history=True snapshots the solved state of every contact at the end of the step (multiplier, penalty stiffness, normal) and restores it onto the matched contacts at the next step’s contact refresh: the normal multiplier along the new normal and, with "latest" matching, the tangential multiplier projected onto the friction disk. Unmatched contacts start cold, and so does every contact when two detection passes run between two steps. Between iterations, a RIGID slot on the ITERATIONS schedule re-detects inside the iteration loop and replaces the contact set in the middle of the solve; the solver snapshots the in-flight state before each pass and restores it onto the new set in the same way, so the multipliers built up in the earlier iterations survive the refresh. The first use is opt-in; the second is not. The constructor therefore raises ValueError when the RIGID slot is on ITERATIONS and the pipeline has "disabled" matching, and also when rigid_contact_history=True meets an owned pipeline without matching.

For CUDA graph capture keep the same order, pipeline, contacts, solver, all before capture. Body-body contact state is pre-sized from the pipeline’s published rigid contact maximum and body-particle contact state from the particle-shape pair count; both grow from Contacts on the first step. During capture that growth needs Warp’s stream-ordered memory pool, otherwise the solver raises and asks you to pre-size. rigid_contact_history=True needs its warm-start buffers allocated before capture, on every device: construct the CollisionPipeline before the solver, which publishes rigid_contact_max, or run one uncaptured step first.

Start at dt = 1/600 s with 10 iterations and adjust following Rules of Thumb. The VBD Tuning parameter table lists the constructor arguments that matter most for tuning, with their effect and cost; the signature of SolverVBD is the complete list.

Model Inputs#

The solver reads three systems from the Model:

  • Cloth: particles with triangles and bending edges, from add_cloth_mesh(), add_cloth_grid(), or add_triangle() and add_edge(). Material: tri_ke, tri_ka and tri_kd per triangle, edge_ke and edge_kd per edge, and particle_radius per particle. validate_mesh=True runs the element-quality checker.

  • Soft bodies: particles with tetrahedra, from add_soft_mesh(), add_soft_grid() or add_tetrahedron(). Material: k_mu, k_lambda and k_damp per tetrahedron. The surface triangles take part in self-contact and rigid-soft contact.

  • Rigid bodies: add_body() with shapes, and joints of type BALL, FIXED, FREE, REVOLUTE, PRISMATIC, D6 and ROD (add_joint_rod() for cables). DISTANCE joints are not supported. Shape materials ke, kd, mu and margin come from ShapeConfig. Drives and limits read joint_target_ke, joint_target_kd, joint_limit_lower, joint_limit_upper, joint_limit_ke and joint_limit_kd.

Springs (add_spring()) and particle-body attachments (add_attachment_particle_body()) connect the systems. The contact material between particles and shapes mixes soft_contact_ke, soft_contact_kd and soft_contact_mu on the Model with the shape values (The energy terms). The Model Parameters table of VBD Tuning gives the effect and typical values of each of these.

Rigid constraint rows use the compliant ALM formulation by default (rigid_compliant_alm=True): the authored stiffness is the physical stiffness of the row and the solver picks the metric \(\rho\) itself (Rigid bodies and joints). The legacy AVBD path (rigid_compliant_alm=False) is deprecated since 1.6, together with its controls: the per-joint vbd:joint_is_hard custom attribute (model.vbd.joint_is_hard, declared by register_custom_attributes()) and set_joint_constraint_mode(). Under compliant ALM they have no solver-mode effect.

Scene values apply in both constraint modes: they are not skipped when the deprecated rigid_compliant_alm=False path is selected, even though the schema scopes them to the compliant ALM path.

Coupling Modes#

Inside one solver no coupling setup is needed. Rigid blocks and particle blocks are solved in the same iteration loop, rigid blocks first, and rigid-soft contacts and springs connect them as ordinary energy terms, and particle-body attachments as compliant augmented-Lagrangian rows (How it is solved). Cloth between the fingers of a VBD-integrated gripper is the plain case.

With integrate_with_external_rigid_solver=True the rigid bodies belong to another solver. SolverVBD then skips the rigid solve, reads the body poses the external solver wrote into the output state, and applies rigid-soft contact to the particles only (one-way coupling). Attachments are not supported in this mode. For two-way coupling through proxies, including contact forces reported back to the source body, see Coupled Solvers.

Contact Handling#

The contact stack has three layers.

Detection. Self-contact pairs come from the triangle-mesh self-contact detector: vertex-triangle and edge-edge candidates within particle_self_contact_margin + particle_self_contact_gap, stored in fixed buffers of particle_vertex_contact_buffer_size and particle_edge_contact_buffer_size entries, and filtered by topological distance (particle_topological_contact_filter_threshold) and rest-shape distance (particle_rest_shape_contact_exclusion_radius). It runs on the schedule of the SOFT_SELF_CONTACT collision slot, by default before and after the inertial step of every substep. Rigid-soft records (particles, and with enable_rigid_soft_full_surface_contact also soft edges and faces) and body-body records come from the CollisionPipeline, which detects out to its soft_contact_gap beyond particle_radius plus the shape margin and carries the shape thicknesses of body-body pairs. With a solver-owned pipeline the rigid slot follows collision_frequency_type as well.

Forces. Each contact kind has its own law with friction and damping; self-contact, rigid-soft contact and body-body contact are given in The energy terms. Full-surface mesh records normally produce force only when they pass the feature-region test of Offset Geometric Contact (mesh_contact_valid); recovery records and rows stored after an overflow bypass the test, and every stored record is kept for truncation.

Truncation. Divide and Truncate keeps self-contact, and with rigid_soft_enable_dat also rigid-soft contact, free of new penetrations between detections, through the planar and the radius truncation of Penetration-free truncation (DAT). The guarantee holds for pairs that were separated at detection and found within the query radius; the rigid trajectory check is sampled unless rigid_soft_dat_use_interval_arithmetic is on.

How to set the distances, and what they cost, is on the tuning page: Contact Handling and Setting margin and gap. The contact methods are described in:

  • Anka H. Chen, Jerry Hsu, Youssef Ayman, and Miles Macklin. 2026. Divide and Truncate: A Penetration and Inversion Free Framework for Coupled Multi-physics System. In ACM SIGGRAPH 2026 Conference Papers. https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811143

  • Anka He Chen, Jerry Hsu, Ziheng Liu, Miles Macklin, Yin Yang, and Cem Yuksel. 2025. Offset Geometric Contact. ACM Trans. Graph. 44, 4. https://doi.org/10.1145/3731205

Key Constructor Parameters#

The constructor has three groups. Common: iterations, friction_epsilon, soft_contact_damping_bidirectional, dat_conservative_bound_relaxation, integrate_with_external_rigid_solver, and collision_pipeline with collision_frequency_type and collision_frequency. particle_*: self-contact on or off, margin and gap, the candidate buffer sizes, the topological and rest-shape filters, and the tile solve. rigid_*: the compliant ALM switch with its stabilization and retention factors, body-body contact history and two-sided damping (rigid_contact_damping_bidirectional), the per-body contact list capacities, the rigid-soft barrier law and DAT, and the structural joint stiffness and damping. Defaults and exact semantics are in SolverVBD; recommended values for particle systems, and effects and symptoms for rigid bodies and cables, are in the VBD Tuning parameter table.

How VBD Works#

VBD (Vertex Block Descent) treats every time step as one minimization and solves it one small block at a time. Two kinds of blocks exist. A particle \(i\) has 3 degrees of freedom, its position \(\mathbf{x}_i\), and a mass \(m_i\). A rigid body \(b\) has 6 degrees of freedom, its pose made of a centre of mass \(\mathbf{c}_b\) and a rotation \(\mathbf{R}_b\), with a mass \(m_b\) and an inertia tensor \(\mathbf{I}_b\). The notation follows the VBD paper cited above: \(h\) is the time step and \(\mathbf{y}\) the inertial prediction. The kernels named on this page live in newton/_src/solvers/vbd/ (solver_vbd.py, particle_vbd_kernels.py, rigid_vbd_kernels.py); they are internal and can change without notice.

Step objective#

Every time step is one minimization, solved one block at a time. With the blocks and notation above, the step finds the positions and poses that minimize

\[G = \sum_i \frac{m_i}{2h^2}\,\lVert \mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{y}_i \rVert^2 + \sum_b \left[ \frac{m_b}{2h^2}\,\lVert \mathbf{c}_b - \mathbf{c}_b^* \rVert^2 + \frac{1}{2h^2}\,\boldsymbol{\theta}_b^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{I}_b\, \boldsymbol{\theta}_b \right] + \sum_e E_e .\]

The first sum is the particle inertia. Its target is the inertial prediction

\[\mathbf{y}_i = \mathbf{x}_i + h\left(\mathbf{v}_i + h\left(\mathbf{g} + \mathbf{f}^{\text{ext}}_i / m_i\right)\right),\]

computed by the forward_step kernel from the current velocity, gravity and external forces. The second sum is the rigid inertia. Its target pose \((\mathbf{c}_b^*, \mathbf{R}_b^*)\) comes from forward integration (forward_step_rigid_bodies), and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_b\) is the rotation vector from the current rotation to the target rotation (solve_rigid_body). The last sum is every energy term in the scene: membrane, bending and volumetric elasticity, self-contact and rigid-soft contact with friction and damping, springs, and the compliant augmented-Lagrangian rows of joints, body-body contacts and particle-body attachments.

How it is solved#

The solver never forms the full system. In each iteration it visits every block once, with the neighbours frozen at their current values. For a block it assembles the gradient \(\mathbf{g}_i\) and the Hessian \(\mathbf{H}_i\) of the terms that touch the block, and moves the block by one Newton step:

\[\Delta \mathbf{x}_i = -\mathbf{H}_i^{-1}\,\mathbf{g}_i, \qquad \mathbf{H}_i = \frac{m_i}{h^2}\,\mathbf{1} + \sum_{e \ni i} \frac{\partial^2 E_e}{\partial \mathbf{x}_i^2}.\]

For a particle this is a 3x3 solve (solve_elasticity). For a body it is a 6x6 solve with the block Hessian \(\operatorname{diag}(m_b/h^2\,\mathbf{1}_3,\ \mathbf{I}_b/h^2)\) plus the energy terms, factored once per visit (solve_rigid_body, ldlt6_solve). The energy blocks of \(\mathbf{H}_i\) are the usual stabilised second derivatives, not always the exact ones: the membrane block is projected to be positive semi-definite, the bending block keeps only the Gauss-Newton term \(k\,\nabla\theta\,\nabla\theta^{\mathsf T}\), and the friction block holds the contact normal fixed. Blocks are grouped into colours so that no two blocks of one colour share a mesh element: a triangle, a tetrahedron, a bending edge when color() is called with include_bending=True, or a joint for bodies. Contact pairs are not part of the colouring, so two blocks of one colour can be in contact; they then update in Jacobi fashion. The update is Gauss-Seidel across colours and Jacobi inside a colour. Rigid blocks are solved before particle blocks in every iteration.

The real call tree, with the control flow written out:

SolverVBD.step(state_in, state_out, control, contacts, dt)
  _initialize_rigid_bodies   -> forward_step_rigid_bodies   (predicted poses)
  _initialize_particles      -> forward_step                (predicted positions y)
  _initialize_particle_body_attachments -> step_particle_body_attachment_lambda_rho
                             (attachment rho from the endpoints' support; retained
                              multipliers scaled by rigid_avbd_gamma)
  collision detection (default: self-contact before and after this initialization;
                       rigid contacts before it only, or before and after it with rigid-soft DAT)
  for iteration in range(iterations):
      if detection is due in this iteration (ITERATIONS schedule only):
          detect again, refresh the DAT reference
      _solve_rigid_body_iteration
          for colour in body colours:
              accumulate attachments, body-particle contacts, body-body contacts
              solve_rigid_body            (6x6 LDL^T per body)
          update dual variables of the joint and contact rows
      _solve_particle_iteration
          for colour in particle colours:
              accumulate attachment, body-particle contact, spring and self-contact terms
              solve_elasticity            (3x3 per particle: inertia + membrane + bending + volumetric)
              DAT: planar truncation per pair, then the radius cap
      update_duals_particle_body_attachments   (dual step of the attachment rows,
                                                after both primal blocks)
  _finalize_rigid_bodies     -> update_body_velocity
  _finalize_particles        -> update_velocity             v = (x_new - x_old) / dt

Two facts follow directly. Information moves about one ring of neighbours per iteration: a block sees only the current values of its neighbours, so a change reaches a neighbour in the same iteration when that neighbour’s colour comes later in the sweep, and in the next iteration otherwise. And an unfinished solve is not discarded: whatever position the last iteration leaves becomes the new velocity through \(\mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{x}_{\text{new}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{old}})/h\).

Rigid bodies and joints#

A rigid body \(b\) is one block with 6 degrees of freedom: the position \(\mathbf{c}_b\) of its centre of mass and a rotation vector about it. With the target pose \((\mathbf{c}_b^*, \mathbf{R}_b^*)\) from forward integration and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_b\) the body-frame rotation vector from the current rotation \(\mathbf{R}_b\) to \(\mathbf{R}_b^*\), the inertia term of the step objective contributes

\[\mathbf{f}^{\text{in}}_b = \frac{m_b}{h^2}\,(\mathbf{c}_b^* - \mathbf{c}_b), \qquad \boldsymbol{\tau}^{\text{in}}_b = \frac{1}{h^2}\,\mathbf{R}_b\,\mathbf{I}_b\,\boldsymbol{\theta}_b, \qquad \mathbf{H}^{\text{in}}_{ll} = \frac{m_b}{h^2}\,\mathbf{1}, \qquad \mathbf{H}^{\text{in}}_{aa} = \frac{1}{h^2}\,\mathbf{R}_b\,\mathbf{I}_b\,\mathbf{R}_b^{\mathsf T}.\]

Every term that touches the body (joints, body-body contacts, rigid-soft contacts, attachments, tendons) adds its force, its torque and the three \(3 \times 3\) blocks of the Hessian. The block then solves one \(6 \times 6\) system by \(LDL^{\mathsf T}\) (ldlt6_solve) and applies the increments (solve_rigid_body; the rotation increment acts in world space and is applied as a normalised small-angle quaternion, which approximates the exponential map):

\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{H}_{ll} & \mathbf{H}_{al}^{\mathsf T} \\ \mathbf{H}_{al} & \mathbf{H}_{aa} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta\mathbf{c} \\ \Delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{f} \\ \boldsymbol{\tau} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{c}_b \leftarrow \mathbf{c}_b + \Delta\mathbf{c}, \qquad \mathbf{R}_b \leftarrow \Delta\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{R}_b, \qquad \Delta\mathbf{R} = \operatorname{normalize}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta},\ 1\big) \approx \exp(\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}) .\end{split}\]

Joints and body-body contacts are constraint rows between two bodies, handled as compliant augmented-Lagrangian (ALM) rows. A row has a residual \(C\), a material stiffness \(k\), an internal metric \(\rho\) that the solver sets when it initialises the row (once per step for joints, at every rigid contact detection for body-body contacts), and a multiplier \(\lambda\). With rigid_avbd_alpha above zero the residual is stabilised to \(C - \alpha C_0\); the default under compliant ALM is zero, and the formulas below use that default. Its force along the row and the dual update after every iteration are

\[f = k_{\text{eff}}\,C + s\,\lambda, \qquad k_{\text{eff}} = \frac{k\,\rho}{k + \rho}, \qquad s = \frac{k}{k + \rho}, \qquad \lambda \leftarrow s\,(\lambda + \rho\,C),\]

and \(k_{\text{eff}}\) enters the Hessian blocks through the row’s Jacobian. A bilateral row keeps the full force. A unilateral row keeps only the admissible sign: for contact \(f_n = \max(f, 0)\) and \(\lambda_n \leftarrow \max(\,\cdot\,, 0)\). Joint multipliers persist across steps, scaled by rigid_avbd_gamma (0.999); body-body contacts restart theirs at zero unless rigid_contact_history=True.

Joints (evaluate_joint_force_hessian). The structural rows of a joint between parent \(p\) and child \(c\) are the offset of the two joint anchors and the rotation away from the rest relative orientation, each projected onto the locked axes of the joint type (compute_kappa_and_jacobian, build_joint_projectors). D6 joints with two or three angular axes are the exception: their angular rows use the absolute joint coordinates of eval_fk in the joint-axis basis (compute_joint_kappa_and_jacobian):

\[\mathbf{C}_{\text{lin}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{lin}}\,(\mathbf{x}_c - \mathbf{x}_p), \qquad \mathbf{C}_{\text{ang}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{ang}}\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\mathbf{R}_p, \mathbf{R}_c), \qquad \mathbf{P} = \mathbf{1} - \sum_{\text{free axes}} \mathbf{a}\,\mathbf{a}^{\mathsf T},\]

so BALL keeps the three linear rows; REVOLUTE the three linear rows and two angular rows; PRISMATIC two linear rows and the three angular rows; FIXED all six; D6 whatever its free axes leave; FREE adds no row. The material stiffness is rigid_joint_linear_ke or rigid_joint_angular_ke, and the metric is \(\rho = \max(\text{support}, 9k)\), chosen so that \(k_{\text{eff}} \ge 0.9\,k\) in every local solve; rigid_joint_linear_kd and rigid_joint_angular_kd add absolute damping. Drives are bilateral rows on the free axes of REVOLUTE, PRISMATIC and D6 joints, with the spring and the damper folded into one row:

\[K = k_t + \frac{d_t}{h}, \qquad e = \frac{k_t\,(q - q^*) + d_t\,(\dot q - \dot q^*)}{K}, \qquad f_d = K_{\text{eff}}\,e + s\,\lambda_d,\]

with \(k_t = \mathtt{joint\_target\_ke}\), \(d_t = \mathtt{joint\_target\_kd}\), and \(K_{\text{eff}}, s\) formed from \(K\) and the axis metric as above. Limits are unilateral rows at the nearer bound with joint_limit_ke and joint_limit_kd; only forces into the bound are kept, and the multiplier is projected onto its half-line. ROD joints use four rows, stretch, shear, bend and twist, with joint_target_ke and joint_target_kd as their stiffness and damping. DISTANCE joints are not supported.

Body-body contact is the unilateral row of the same family: \(C\) is the penetration of the two effective surfaces, \(k\) the pair average of the shape stiffness, \(\rho_n\) the pair’s inverse Delassus support over \(h^2\), \(f_n = \max(k_{\text{eff}}\,C + s\,\lambda_n,\ 0)\), and friction is a Coulomb projection carrying \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t\). The complete law, with the material mixing and the damping, is in The energy terms.

The deprecated legacy path (rigid_compliant_alm=False) replaces the finite-material rows with ramped penalties and hard or soft slots.

Elasticity is three terms. The membrane term resists stretch and shear of a triangle. With \(\mathbf{F}\) the in-plane deformation gradient, \(I_C = \lVert \mathbf{F} \rVert^2\) and \(J_s\) the area ratio, the energy per unit rest area is the stable Neo-Hookean membrane energy

\[\psi_{\text{m}} = \frac{\mu}{2}\,(I_C - 2) + \frac{\lambda_{\text{NH}}}{2}\,(J_s - \alpha)^2, \qquad \alpha = 1 + \frac{\mu}{\lambda_{\text{NH}}},\]

with \(\mu = \mathtt{tri\_ke}\) and \(\lambda_{\text{NH}} = \mathtt{tri\_ka} + \mathtt{tri\_ke}\); the triangle energy is \(A_0\,\psi_{\text{m}}\). tri_kd damps the rate of the metric \(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{F}\), so rigid rotations are not damped. The bending term resists a change of the dihedral angle \(\theta\) across an interior edge of rest length \(L_0\):

\[E_{\text{b}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathtt{edge\_ke}\;L_0\,(\theta - \theta_0)^2,\]

and edge_kd damps the angle rate with the force \(\mathbf{f}_d = -\mathtt{edge\_kd}\,L_0\,\dot\theta\,\partial\theta/\partial\mathbf{x}\), where \(\dot\theta\) is the change of \(\theta\) over the step divided by \(h\). The volumetric term is the stable Neo-Hookean energy of a tetrahedron of rest volume \(V_0\),

\[\psi_{\text{v}} = \frac{\mu}{2}\,(I_C - 3) + \frac{\lambda_{\text{NH}}}{2}\,(J - \alpha)^2, \qquad J = \det \mathbf{F},\]

with \(\mu = \mathtt{k\_mu}\), \(\lambda_{\text{NH}} = \mathtt{k\_lambda} + \mathtt{k\_mu}\), the same \(\alpha\), energy \(V_0\,\psi_{\text{v}}\), and k_damp damping the rate of the metric \(\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{F}\) like tri_kd. In both Neo-Hookean terms the user values are Lamé parameters; the kernels convert them to the Neo-Hookean parameters, which is why \(\lambda_{\text{NH}}\) carries the extra \(\mu\).

Contact is three different terms, one per contact kind. They share the friction smoothing length \(\epsilon_u = \mathtt{friction\_epsilon}\, h\) and nothing else: each kind has its own distance, its own stiffness and its own law.

Self-contact acts between a vertex and a triangle, or between two edges, of one particle system (accumulate_self_contact_force_and_hessian). The distance \(d\) is the closest-point distance of the pair, the interaction distance is \(r = \mathtt{particle\_self\_contact\_margin}\) and the stiffness is \(k = \mathtt{soft\_contact\_ke}\). The energy (evaluate_self_contact_force_norm) is a quadratic penalty that turns into a logarithmic barrier at half the interaction distance:

\[\begin{split}E_{\text{sc}}(d) = \begin{cases} \tfrac{k}{2}\,(r - d)^2 & \tfrac{r}{2} \le d < r,\\[3pt] -k\,(\tfrac{r}{2})^2 \ln d + \text{const} & d_{\min} < d < \tfrac{r}{2},\\[3pt] \text{second-order expansion of the barrier at } d_{\min} & d \le d_{\min}, \end{cases} \qquad d_{\min} = \min(10\,\mu\text{m},\ \tfrac{r}{4}).\end{split}\]

The gradient in the barrier region is \(-k\,(r/2)^2/d\), so the force grows like \(1/d\) as the gap closes; below \(d_{\min}\) it continues linearly, so the force stays finite. A pair whose closest points coincide (\(d = 0\)) is skipped. The pair force is distributed to the four vertices by the barycentric weights of the two closest points. Friction follows the IPC form (compute_friction). With \(f_n = -\partial E_{\text{sc}}/\partial d\) the normal force, \(\mathbf{u}\) the tangential relative displacement of the two closest points over the step and \(\mathbf{T}\) the tangent basis,

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{f}_{\text{fr}} = -\mu\, f_n\, \mathbf{T}\, \frac{f_1(\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert)}{\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert}\,\mathbf{u}, \qquad f_1(x) = \begin{cases} 2x/\epsilon_u - x^2/\epsilon_u^2 & x < \epsilon_u,\\ 1 & x \ge \epsilon_u, \end{cases}\end{split}\]

with \(\mu = \mathtt{soft\_contact\_mu}\). Unlike IPC, the contact normal is held constant inside the block solve; the kernel notes that this improves stability. Damping (damp_collision) acts on the gap rate \(\dot d = (d - d_{\text{prev}})/h\), the change of the contact distance over the substep; \(d_{\text{prev}}\) is the distance at the substep-start positions from its own closest point, so a rigid motion of the pair gives \(\dot d = 0\). The force is \(\mathbf{f}_d = -\mathtt{soft\_contact\_kd}\;\dot d\,\mathbf{n}\) with the Hessian \((\mathtt{soft\_contact\_kd}/h)\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^T\). By default (soft_contact_damping_bidirectional=True) it acts while the gap closes and while it opens. soft_contact_damping_bidirectional=False keeps closing-only damping, which leaves a separating pair undamped.

Rigid-soft contact acts between a soft contact point and one shape of a rigid or static body. The point is a particle, or, with full-surface mesh contact (enable_rigid_soft_full_surface_contact), a barycentric point on a soft edge or face; the records come from the collision pipeline. The distance is \(d = \mathbf{n}\cdot(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b})\), with \(\mathbf{b}\) the shape’s contact point carried along with its body and \(\mathbf{n}\) the stored contact normal. The interaction distance is \(r = \mathtt{particle\_radius}\) plus the shape margin; an edge or face point uses the largest radius among its corners. The material is mixed per contact from the two sides (init_body_particle_contacts): \(k = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathtt{soft\_contact\_ke} + \mathtt{ke}_{\text{shape}})\), \(k_d = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathtt{soft\_contact\_kd} + \mathtt{kd}_{\text{shape}})\) and \(\mu = \sqrt{\mathtt{soft\_contact\_mu}\;\mathtt{mu}_{\text{shape}}}\). The normal law is the plain quadratic penalty \(E = \tfrac{k}{2}(r - d)^2\) for \(d < r\); rigid_soft_contact_use_log_barrier=True switches it to the self-contact barrier law. Damping and friction have the same form as for self-contact, written in world space (compute_projected_isotropic_friction): the damping force \(-k_d\,\dot d\,\mathbf{n}\), with \(\dot d = \mathbf{n}\cdot\Delta\mathbf{x}_{\text{rel}}/h\) the normal rate of the point relative to the shape, acts in both directions by default, or only while the point approaches the shape with soft_contact_damping_bidirectional=False, and the friction force is \(-\mu\, f_n\, f_1(\lVert\mathbf{u}_t\rVert)\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t\), with \(\mathbf{u}_t\) the tangential slip of the point relative to the shape surface over the step. The force is distributed to the particles by their barycentric weights, and the equal and opposite force with its torque acts on the body (accumulate_body_particle_contacts_per_body). With full-surface mesh contact, every stored edge and face record is kept for DAT, but normally only the records that pass the feature-region test of Offset Geometric Contact produce force (mesh_contact_valid); penetration-recovery records and rows stored after a buffer overflow bypass the test.

Body-body contact acts between two shapes, on two rigid bodies or on a rigid body and a static shape. It is a constraint row of the rigid solve, not a penalty energy. The gap is measured between the two effective surfaces: with \(\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1\) the support points carried along with their bodies and \(t_0, t_1\) the effective thicknesses (shape radius plus margin, stored by the collision pipeline as rigid_contact_margin0 and rigid_contact_margin1), the penetration is

\[C = -\big(\mathbf{n}\cdot(\mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_0) - t_0 - t_1\big).\]

The material is the pair average of the shape values: \(k = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathtt{ke}_0 + \mathtt{ke}_1)\), \(k_d = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathtt{kd}_0 + \mathtt{kd}_1)\) and \(\mu = \sqrt{\mathtt{mu}_0\,\mathtt{mu}_1}\). On the default compliant ALM path (rigid_compliant_alm=True) the normal force is

\[f_n = \max\!\big(k_{\text{eff}}\,C + s\,\lambda_n,\ 0\big), \qquad k_{\text{eff}} = \frac{k\,\rho_n}{k + \rho_n}, \qquad s = \frac{k}{k + \rho_n},\]

where \(\lambda_n\) is the multiplier of the row and \(\rho_n\) is an internal metric that the solver sets every step from the pair’s inverse Delassus support over \(h^2\) plus structural stiffness; the user does not set \(\rho_n\). The force acts along \(\mathbf{n}\) at the support points with the matching torques, and the row adds \(k_{\text{eff}}\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^{\mathsf T}\) to the body Hessians (evaluate_rigid_contact_from_collision). After every iteration the multiplier is advanced by the relaxed ascent \(\lambda_n \leftarrow \max\big(s\,(\lambda_n + \rho_n C),\ 0\big)\) (update_duals_body_body_contacts). At the fixed point \(\lambda_n = k\,C\) and \(f_n = k\,C\): a converged resting contact carries its load like a penalty of the material stiffness \(k\), with the penetration \(f_n/k\), while each single iteration uses the smaller \(k_{\text{eff}}\), which keeps the 6x6 systems well conditioned. Friction is a Coulomb bound, not a smooth law: the tangential force \(\rho_t\,\mathbf{u}_t + \boldsymbol{\lambda}_t\), with \(\mathbf{u}_t\) the tangential offset between the two anchored surface points and \(\rho_t\) the tangent metric, is projected onto the disk \(\lVert\mathbf{f}_t\rVert \le \mu f_n\), and \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_t\) is advanced and projected onto the disk of radius \(\mu\lambda_n\) in the same dual step. Damping is \(-k_d\,(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}})\,\mathbf{n}\) while the row carries load, only while the bodies approach by default and in both directions of the normal relative velocity with rigid_contact_damping_bidirectional=True. The multipliers start from zero in every step unless rigid_contact_history=True warm-starts them from the matched contacts of the previous step. The legacy AVBD path (rigid_compliant_alm=False, deprecated) uses the pair stiffness as a fixed penalty by default (rigid_avbd_beta is zero). With a positive beta (rigid_avbd_linear_beta, falling back to rigid_avbd_beta) the penalty starts at rigid_contact_k_start and grows by beta times the penetration per iteration, capped at the pair stiffness. Its soft mode uses the IPC friction law instead of the Coulomb projection.

The underlying methods are described in the references cited by SolverVBD: VBD for the particle blocks, AVBD for the rigid rows, Divide and Truncate (DAT) for penetration-free contact and Offset Geometric Contact (OGC) for the feature regions of mesh contact. The two contact papers are also listed under Contact Handling above.

Particle-body attachments are compliant augmented-Lagrangian rows of the same form as the joint rows (Rigid bodies and joints). With \(C = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{a}\) the offset of the particle from its anchor point fixed on the body, the row applies \(k_{\text{eff}}\,C + s\,\lambda\) to the particle and the opposite force, with its torque, to the body; \(k_{\text{eff}}\) and \(s\) follow from the authored stiffness \(k\) and the metric \(\rho\) as above. The solver sets \(\rho\) once per step to the row’s support, the endpoints’ reduced inverse mass over \(h^2\) plus the particle’s incident material Hessian, and at the same time scales the retained multiplier by rigid_avbd_gamma (step_particle_body_attachment_lambda_rho). The multiplier advances once per iteration after both primal blocks, \(\lambda \leftarrow s\,(\lambda + \rho\,C)\) (update_duals_particle_body_attachments). Infinite stiffness is the hard limit of the same row, \(k_{\text{eff}} = \rho\) and \(s = 1\), and ignores the attachment damping; a finite stiffness keeps its spring offset under load and adds the damping force \(k_d\,\dot C\). Springs between two particles stay plain penalty energies (accumulate_spring_force_and_hessian).

Penetration-free truncation (DAT)#

Self-contact and rigid-soft contact are penalty energies, so a large step could jump through a surface between two detections. The Divide and Truncate scheme (DAT) prevents that for every pair that detection found separated and within the query radius. It works on the displacement each vertex has accumulated since the last detection and has two distinct parts.

Planar truncation acts per detected vertex-triangle or edge-edge pair (apply_planar_truncation_parallel_by_collision). At the detection-time positions a certified separating plane is found and placed between the two primitives; it splits the gap in proportion to how far each side wants to move toward the other, and each side keeps at least 5 percent of the gap (place_dat_division_plane). Each vertex of the pair receives a factor \(t \le 1\) so that its straight trajectory stays on its own side of the plane, scaled by dat_conservative_bound_relaxation; motion away from the plane or along it passes untouched (planar_truncation_t). A vertex in several pairs keeps the smallest factor. A pair whose primitives already intersect at detection time has no strict separator and is left unconstrained in that pass.

Radius truncation acts on every particle (apply_truncation_ts). After the planar factor is applied, the length of the accumulated displacement is capped at one half of dat_conservative_bound_relaxation times the query radius, isotropically. Two primitives farther apart than the query radius at detection therefore cannot meet before the next detection.

Rigid-soft DAT (rigid_soft_enable_dat, off by default) applies the same two parts to contacts between soft geometry and shapes. One thread per contact record builds the division plane from the detection-time configuration and truncates both sides against it (apply_rigid_soft_truncation). The soft vertices of the record move on straight rays, as in self-contact. The vertices of the rigid primitive move on curved trajectories because the body rotates, so their crossing times are found by sampling and bisection, with an interval bound on the signed-distance derivative for rows that start inside the plane’s band; rigid_soft_dat_use_interval_arithmetic additionally certifies the sampled prefix with interval arithmetic (experimental). Without it, a crossing that happens and reverses between two sample points can be missed. Full-surface mesh contacts name the exact rigid triangle, edge or vertex through soft_contact_rigid_indices; analytic SDF contacts and penetration-recovery contacts truncate only their stored rigid surface point. Each body then scales its whole pose update, translation and rotation about the centre of mass, by the smallest factor of its contacts (apply_body_truncation_ts). The radius truncation caps the motion of every point of the body, bounded by the translation plus the rotation angle times the body’s bounding radius, at one half of the relaxation factor times the smallest rigid-soft query radius (pipeline gap plus soft feature radius plus shape margin); that budget is fixed at construction. The pass runs after the forward step and after every body colour of every iteration, and the particle pass re-enforces the same planes after the particles move. Kinematic bodies move outside the solver and are not truncated. A pair for which no separating plane can be certified freezes. Contact Handling describes the parts for tuning and illustrates the planes.

The guarantee has a price: a vertex cannot move faster than about one motion budget per detection cycle. The motion that the truncation removes is called DAT damping here and in VBD Tuning. It is not related to any kd damping coefficient.
